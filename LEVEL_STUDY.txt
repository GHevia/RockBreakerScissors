Rock Breaker Scissors: campaign progression and playability study

The campaign stays at 100 levels. Levels 1–10 are lessons, followed by the
slower opening layouts at levels 11–15. The five additional lessons replace
five late review layouts; the two existing 99/100 finales are preserved.
The user's level 5 layout, including the moving paper in the lower lane,
is unchanged.

Progression
-----------
1       Aim and the goal: turn every object into rock.
2       Bouncing rocks and multiple targets.
3       Moving targets and leading a shot.
4       Paper/scissors conversion with a forgiving setup.
5       Moving shots retain their motion when converted; cross-lane practice.
6       The all-scissors deadline: the pre-shot exception ends here.
7       Put a rock in play while scissors are still clearing paper.
8       Keep scissors alive: only rocks and paper is a lost cause.
9       Deliver paper to stationary scissors; keep a backup rock alive.
10      Put the timing and population rules together.
11–15   Easy play: 2–4 large targets, 100–120 speed, eight shots, no walls.
16–25   Faster movement: 130–166 speed, large targets, eight shots.
26–35   Smaller targets: radius 18, six shots, no walls or starting rocks.
36–45   Starting rocks: protect scissors from existing rocks; six shots.
46–55   Walls: dividers, shelves, gates; five shots and normal-sized targets.
56–65   Stationary puzzles: includes fully stationary paper/scissors boards.
66–75   Mixed sizes and speeds: combine the previously learned mechanics.
76–85   Fewer shots: four shots, combined wall and trajectory puzzles.
86–95   Crowded arenas: more populations to track, with four shots.
96–100  Final combinations, ending in the open-lane 99/100 finales.

New-mechanic instructions appear at 16, 26, 36, 46, 56, 66, 76, 86, and 96.
Instructions pause play and remain until acknowledged or skipped. Selecting
any lesson explicitly always shows its text, even with level-100 progress.
Continue remembers acknowledged lessons; Restart does not repeat them. Help
reopens the current instructions. Existing unlock progress is preserved.

Rules and stationary puzzles
----------------------------
A stationary paper/scissors board is NOT inherently impossible. Shoot a rock
into paper: that projectile retains its velocity as paper. It can then reach
stationary scissors, become moving scissors, and convert other paper. The
audit searches this strategy; levels 56–59 preserve fully stationary puzzles.

Only lessons 1–5 allow a scissors-only arena before the first shot. From
lesson 6 onward, including random rounds, all scissors loses immediately,
even before the player fires. All paper always loses. Regular levels that
started with only scissors (now 23–25) have paper added. Mixed rocks and
paper do not trigger a loss screen: gameplay continues until a uniform
outcome. The offline solution search may prune that mathematically unwinnable
branch, but this is not a gameplay loss condition.

Audit method and results
------------------------
Run from this checkout:
    python3 scripts/audit_levels.py --check --update-test-sequences

Requires Python 3 and a C++17 compiler, not Xcode. The script reads the actual
authored Swift definitions and compiles an offline model of GameScene's
fixed 1/60-second movement, radius scale, launch position, boundaries, wall
bounces, conversion order, ammunition, and terminal conditions.

Each level is checked at arena sizes 320x568, 390x600, and 768x900. The search
tries delayed shots where the board permits them, early shots to preserve
a rock before paper disappears, and paper deliveries with backup rocks. Winning sequences have at most the available ammunition
and run for at most 120 simulated seconds. These are example solutions,
not optimal solutions or measured human difficulty scores.

Result: 300/300 level/size combinations have a recorded simulated win.
No startup object/object or object/wall overlaps were found. No layout loses
immediately at startup. Under the stricter all-scissors rule, some layouts
now require quick shots. idle_loss_seconds records the first uniform loss
within a three-second no-input check; -1 means no loss during that window.
Instruction reading is paused and costs no game time. Winning sequences
prove simulated solvability, not a comfortable human reaction window.

The initial audit found startup overlaps in 18 level/size combinations,
including objects inside walls. Those placements were nudged clear while
preserving the surrounding layout. Failure to find a winning sequence is
NOT treated as proof that a puzzle is impossible.

level-audit.csv contains the level parameters, startup findings, win times,
and exact launch frames and normalized aim coordinates for all 300 cases.
The test suite embeds those sequences and replays them through the actual
SpriteKit GameScene, including stationary puzzles. Run the Xcode unit and
UI test targets on a Mac to verify native behavior. This Linux environment
has neither Xcode nor Swift, so native execution and visual QA remain open.

Random mode and the campaign finale
----------------------------------
Winning level 100 shows "You win!", acknowledges completing all 100 campaign
levels, and offers Random Levels, Replay Level, and Main Menu. Completion
is saved independently from highest-unlocked progress.

Random Levels is also available from the main menu without completing the
campaign. Each round has 3–8 objects, both paper and scissors, randomized
positions with jitter, directions, and speeds, eight shots, and no walls.
A shuffled grid keeps starting objects apart on a small arena. Restart
preserves the generated layout; Skip makes a new one immediately. Winning
or skipping random rounds never changes campaign unlock progress. Random
rounds are not solution-certified and may be difficult; Skip stays available.

Version 1.1: three game modes
----------------------------
The main menu now has Rock Mode, Balance, and Bet. Rock Mode contains
Continue, Select Level, Random Levels, and Start Over. This keeps choosing
a game separate from choosing how to enter its campaign. Version metadata
is 1.1, build 2. Campaign layouts and unlock progress are unchanged.

Balance starts with a continuously randomized spread of paper and scissors.
The player fires unlimited rocks from the existing bottom-center launcher.
The round ends when either paper or scissors disappears, regardless of
whether the remaining objects are uniform. Rocks need not remain present:
they are the replenishable input, and the initial population has no rocks.
The timer starts after the instructions are dismissed and measures active
game time. Backgrounding pauses the timer and the simulation. Completed
rounds save the best time separately from Rock Mode progress; resetting
Rock progress does not erase the Balance record.

Bet starts with all three types present, random counts, continuous random
positions, independent random directions, and random speeds. Initial
overlap is avoided by rejection sampling. The arena remains frozen until
the player picks rock, paper, or scissors from the top bar. The choice
locks, 3/2/1 counts down, then the simulation runs without player shots.
A correct prediction displays "You won!". New Round cancels any pending
countdown and generates another board. Backgrounding pauses the countdown
and simulation. No campaign progress is awarded by either new mode.

Daily puzzles remain a possible follow-up, not part of 1.1. A daily puzzle
would need a stable date-based seed, a defined daily timezone, and separate
score records so all players see the same board for the same date. The
random generators accept an injected RNG to support seeded boards later.

Native tests were added for mode generation, balance timing and score
persistence, countdown/choice transitions, frozen bet previews, and mode
isolation. UI tests cover the three-mode navigation and countdown entry.
These require Xcode; this Linux environment cannot execute or visually
verify the new iOS game flows.

1.1 polish
----------
Balance uses radius 18 for initial objects and launched rocks. New Round
within Balance skips the description and immediately starts the next timer;
entering Balance from the main menu still shows the instructions. The end
callback names the missing population(s), and the results card uses a 60%
background so the frozen final arena remains visible in every mode.

Bet controls use a compact footer outside the arena, so the prompt and
choices never cover objects. The footer stays the same height after a pick.
Its header, results, menu description, and settings show wins/completed rounds
and a rounded win percentage. Only resolved rounds count; duplicate result
callbacks and abandoned previews/countdowns do not count. Refresh Betting
Record in Settings clears just those two saved counters.

Arena sizing and readability
---------------------------
Bet now generates 6–11 objects, with radius 18 instead of 14. Balance also
uses radius 18, including fired rocks. Random placement leaves at least
34 points between centers on a 320x252 arena.

SpriteView is constrained to the GeometryReader viewport rather than
expanding into safe areas. GameScene.didChangeSize rebuilds drawn edges
and walls and remaps/clamps existing object positions using their radii.
This also runs during the frozen first Bet preview: a size change cannot
leave the drawn top edge at its initial size while motion uses new bounds.
Resizing does not reset the round, result, timer, or choice. Native tests
cover first-load/resizing bounds, and a UI check keeps the prompt outside
the arena. Running those checks and visual QA still requires Xcode.
